The race isn't about speed any more. A middle layer is missing.

“Nobody has built ‘UPI for stablecoins’, and the missing piece is not a faster chain… What is missing is the layer UPI gets from NPCI: one shared name directory, one request and QR format every wallet accepts, mandatory refund and turnaround rules, and safe mandates.”Report, opening summary

In plain words

Moving dollars on a blockchain is already quick and cheap. What people lack is the agreement on top: a name every app can look up, a QR every wallet can scan, and rules for what happens when a payment goes wrong. In India, NPCI provides exactly that agreement for every bank and app. In crypto, nobody does.

What it means for you

Don't pitch "a faster chain" or "a cheaper transfer". Pitch the rules layer. WondrPay already has two of the four pieces (refund cases and Autopay).

Build the rules layerDon't build another chain

UPI is five shared layers. Crypto has each piece, but locked inside one wallet or one chain.

“Crypto has a live equivalent for most UPI layers, from ENS names to CCTP burn-and-mint, but each one is locked to a chain or a wallet, and the research found no consortium writing a shared rulebook.”Report, opening summary and UPI layer map

In plain words

Think of UPI as five floors of one building that every app shares. Crypto has built each floor separately, but each sits inside a different company's building. MetaMask can't read a MiniPay phone number, and a Solana Pay QR doesn't work in an Ethereum wallet. The top floor, the rulebook that says what happens when money goes wrong, doesn't exist anywhere.

What it means for you

Your column on the right is the plan. The most valuable cell is the bottom one: no one else has a rulebook, and you already store refund cases and receipts on Arkiv.

In UPI the app never touches the money. That is the model to copy.

“UPI is described as a neutral switch where ‘the app does not hold funds, and the bank does not own the user’… Third-party apps such as PhonePe and Google Pay… never hold customer funds.”Report, UPI section, citing NPCI
8 steps
  1. You type your PIN in the PhonePe app. The app only asks; it holds no money.
  2. Your bank checks the PIN and takes the rupees from your account.
  3. NPCI's switch routes the payment and applies the shared rules (limits, refunds).
  4. The shop's bank receives the money.
  5. The shop's app shows "received". Neither app ever held the money.
  6. In WondrPay, your own key (unlocked by PIN or fingerprint) signs the payment.
  7. The money moves on the issuer's own rail straight to the shop's wallet.
  8. WondrPay provides the name lookup, the receipt and the refund rules, but stays out of the money path. That keeps you a software app, not a money transmitter.

In plain words

UPI scaled because apps compete on experience while the money only moves bank to bank. The crypto equivalent: the user's wallet signs, the stablecoin issuer's rail moves the money, and your app provides everything around it.

What it means for you

Your current design (user signs, relayer only pays gas) already follows this. Keep it that way as you add chains. The relayer and escrow contracts still need a lawyer's opinion.

User key signs every legLegal opinion on relayer
Never hold user funds

Payment chains now compete on who pays the network fee.

“Every serious payment chain now finalizes in about a second or less. The real differences are in the gas model and in payment primitives built into the protocol.”Report, chain comparison

In plain words

Every chain except Ethereum and Tron costs less than a cent per transfer. The real difference is what the user must hold to pay the fee. On Arc, Tempo, Stable and Celo it's the dollars they already have; on Plasma, sending USDT is free. Elsewhere they need a second coin (ETH, MON, WBT), unless an app pays for them. Tron is where most USDT is, and it's the most expensive.

What it means for you

Whitechain has no built-in way to pay gas for users, so your relayer is a real feature there, not a workaround. On new chains, prefer ones where fees are paid in stablecoins.

Tempo reserves space in every block just for payments.

“A transaction qualifies for the payment lane only if every call is an allow-listed TIP-20 payment, and a header field caps gas for everything else, so non-payment traffic cannot crowd out payments… Its 32-byte memos and virtual addresses give merchants invoice reconciliation.”Report, newest payment chains

In plain words

On a busy general chain, a payment waits in the same queue as a trading bot. Tempo gives payments a reserved lane in every block, like a bus lane on a highway. Each payment can also carry a short reference (an invoice number), so a shop's books match automatically.

What it means for you

Phase 2 material: when you add merchant reconciliation, Tempo's memos replace the order-id plumbing you built yourself on Whitechain.

Every free transfer is an internal transfer.

“Binance Pay is free only Binance-to-Binance, World Money usernames reach only other World Money users, and Venmo's external crypto sends are irreversible.”Report, consumer apps patterns

In plain words

"Free and instant" in these apps happens because the money never leaves the company's own database. The moment you pay someone in a different app, you're back to copying a long address, picking a chain and paying a fee, with no way back if you get it wrong. Their usernames are a way to keep you in, not a shared directory.

What it means for you

A name that works across apps is your differentiator. Let other wallets look up WondrPay names (the federated resolver in Phase 2), and show a verified recipient name before every send.

Federated name lookupVerified recipient name before paying

Moving dollars between chains: solved for USDC, partly for USDT, risky for everything else.

“CCTP V2 burns and mints native USDC across about 31 domains… Fast Transfer settles in about 8–20 seconds… USDT0 uses LayerZero OFT burn and mint on about 24–25 networks. Tron cannot host native OFT, so it connects only through a liquidity-based ‘Legacy Mesh’.”Report, cross-chain section

In plain words

The safest way to move a stablecoin between chains is for its issuer to destroy it on one chain and create it on the other. Circle does that for USDC and Tether does it for USDT0. Nothing sits in a pool waiting to be stolen. Tron is the exception: its USDT can only be swapped through someone's pool. "Fast" moves only work because someone fronts the money while the slow confirmation finishes.

What it means for you

Use only the issuers' own rails. Never auto-swap USDT into USDC as part of a transfer: that's a trade, with its own risk and licensing.

Circle Gateway + CCTP for USDCUSDT0 for USDT
No own bridge or solverNo auto USDT↔USDC swaps

Why no one offers "one address, any chain": it turns you into a bridge and a money transmitter.

“‘Any chain in, any chain out’ is often a swap plus a bridge, not a transfer… FinCEN treats anyone who ‘accepts CVCs and retransmits them’ as a money transmitter, while unhosted wallet providers generally are not.”Report, gap 1
8 steps
  1. Someone pays your "universal address" with USDT on Tron.
  2. To convert it, a company has to receive that money into something it controls.
  3. Then swap USDT for USDC, which is a trade with a price.
  4. Then move it to another chain over a bridge, which is the most-hacked part of crypto.
  5. Only then does the receiver get USDC on Base. Steps 2–4 are "accept and retransmit": regulated money transmission.
  6. That means licences in every market and permanent compliance costs.
  7. Plus hacks, issuer freezes, and standards that aren't final.
  8. Even the "simple" version breaks: the same address can belong to a stranger on another chain.

In plain words

It isn't that nobody thought of it. A universal address needs a middleman who takes your money on one chain and hands out different money on another. In law that middleman is a money transmitter, which costs millions and years to license, and in practice it's a bridge, which is the most attacked thing in crypto. It only pays off at very large volume.

What it means for you

Promise "pay any WondrPay name from wherever your dollars are", not "one address on every chain". The receiver picks which chain they want, and the issuer rail delivers it, signed by the sender.

Receiver chooses their chainIssuer rails only
Don't promise a magic universal address

No refunds or disputes in crypto, because card fees are what pay for chargebacks.

“Mastercard estimates 261M chargebacks in 2025, worth $33.79B. Large merchants report $151.88 of internal cost per chargeback… Stablecoin acceptance sells on low fees, so no margin exists to fund a dispute desk.”Report, gap 3

In plain words

When you dispute a card payment, someone pays for that process, and it comes out of the 2–3% the shop pays on every sale. Stablecoins are popular because they skip that fee, so nothing is left to pay for refunds. The law doesn't require one either: the US stablecoin law has no error-resolution rule, and Stripe's own stablecoin payments say "Dispute support: No".

What it means for you

This is your strongest opening. You already have refund cases and receipts. Add an escrow checkout (the Coinbase/Shopify pattern) and a small opt-in protection fee that funds a reserve, priced so it doesn't invite fake claims.

Escrow checkoutPublished refund deadlinesOpt-in protection feeTest with 20 merchants

The only pull payment every wallet supports is also the drainer's favourite tool.

“Signature phishing cost about $494M in 2024 and $83.85M in 2025, and Permit/Permit2 accounted for 38% of large-case losses… Even NPCI concluded that P2P pull was too dangerous.”Report, gap 4

In plain words

For a shop to charge you later (a subscription), crypto wallets usually ask you to sign "this address may take my tokens". Drainers trick people into signing exactly that. The safer standard (scoped permissions with limits) is still a draft and only works in a few wallets. UPI went through the same thing and switched off person-to-person pull requests entirely.

What it means for you

Your Autopay already caps total spend on-chain. Add the remaining rules: one merchant, per-pull and monthly caps, a cooling-off period, a public list and one-tap revoke.

WondrPay v2: be the rulebook and the name lookup, and let the issuers move the money.

“The v2 thesis is ‘UPI's rulebook layer for stablecoins’: a non-custodial app plus an open spec… a handle resolver… a signed payment-request object… a receipt, refund and dispute ledger… a mandate registry.”Report, blueprint

In plain words

Picture WondrPay as the "rules and records" company, not the "money moving" company. The four blue boxes are what you own and publish openly. The green row is other people's rails, which the user's own key uses. The bottom row is which chain does which job. That split keeps you legally lighter and safer, and makes you something other wallets can plug into.

What it means for you

Most of the blue boxes exist in some form today. The work is turning them into a written spec other wallets can adopt, then adding Base and Gateway as the first extra chains.

The order to build it in

“Put refund-protected checkout in front of 20 WhiteBIT-corridor merchants and measure whether buyers pay more, return more often, or both. That single number tells you whether the protection fee, the core of the business model, is real.”Report, conclusion

In plain words

Prove the refund idea where you already have a head start (Whitechain and WhiteBIT users), then add chains, then invite other wallets to use your rules. Each phase has one number that says whether it worked.

The first experiment

Refund-protected checkout for 20 merchants. If buyers spend more or come back more often, the protection fee is a real business. If not, you learn it cheaply before building the rest.
